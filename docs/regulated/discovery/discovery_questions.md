# Discovery interview script — 30 minutes

This script is **Mom Test style** (Rob Fitzpatrick, *The Mom
Test*, 2013). Every question is about the **past**, never about
the **future**, and never about the **solution**. Hypotheticals
("would you use a tool that…?") are flattering but useless;
behaviour and concrete pain are useful.

The script is a **menu**, not a sequence. Pick the questions that
match the persona; don't run all of them. Aim for **80% them
talking, 20% you asking**.

---

## A. Setup (≈ 3 minutes)

The first 3 minutes are *not* a pitch. They are calibration:

- Thank them for the 30 minutes.
- One sentence: "I'm researching how labs / QA teams work with
  AI assistants today; I'd love to learn from what you've
  actually run into."
- Ask permission to take notes.
- Confirm: "Is it OK if this stays as research? I'm not
  selling anything." (If they push to know what you do, give
  one sentence and pivot back.)

---

## B. Past behaviour — anchor questions (12–15 to choose from)

> Pick 5–7 from this list. Each question is **a story prompt,
> not a yes/no**. If they answer in two words, follow up with
> "tell me more about that" or "walk me through what happened".

### B1 — present-day reality

1. **"Tell me about the last time an AI tool surprised you in
   your team's workflow — good or bad."**
2. "When was the last time you had to defend a decision that
   was supported by AI output? What did you have to put on the
   table?"
3. "Walk me through the last time you reviewed an audit trail
   for an AI-assisted decision. What was painful?"

### B2 — the pain around evidence and signatures

4. "Tell me about a recent time you had to reconstruct a
   decision six months after the fact — what records did you
   have to gather, where did they live?"
5. "When was the last time an inspection or internal audit
   asked for evidence about a system that was harder than
   expected to produce?"
6. "Tell me about a deviation or OOS where, looking back, the
   audit trail had a gap. What did you do?"

### B3 — the pain around AI specifically

7. "Has anyone in your team used an LLM for SOP drafting,
   analytical interpretation or report writing? What happened
   the first time?"
8. "Tell me about a time when an AI suggestion was *almost*
   right and someone caught it. What kind of review process
   was in place?"
9. "How does your QMS handle the question 'who decided this'
   when the something-something was a model output?"

### B4 — the workaround

10. "How do people in your team currently log that they reviewed
    AI-assisted output? Where does the record live?"
11. "Tell me about a tool or workflow your team built itself —
    spreadsheet, SharePoint, a script — to handle AI evidence."
12. "If a colleague asked you 'how do we govern this AI thing
    safely?' today, what would you tell them, based on what
    you've actually seen work?"

### B5 — the past-future bridge (only if there's time)

13. "What's the next workflow in your team where AI assistance
    would be useful — and what's stopping you from putting it
    in place?"
14. "Last time you blocked an AI-related initiative, what was
    the reason?"
15. "Last time you championed an AI-related initiative, what was
    the deciding factor?"

---

## C. Closing (≈ 3 minutes)

- "Is there someone else you'd recommend I talk to?"
  *(Chasing referrals here is the single highest-value action.)*
- "Can I follow up if I have one specific question by email?"
  *(Sets up the second conversation, much shorter.)*
- "Anything you'd want me to share back when this is more
  developed?" *(Lets them opt in if they want; respects their
  time if they don't.)*

---

## D. Anti-patterns to avoid

- ❌ "Would you pay for X?" / "Would you use a tool that…?" —
  hypothetical; flattering; useless.
- ❌ Showing slides or a demo before listening for at least 20
  minutes.
- ❌ Telling them the problem you think they have. Let them
  tell you. If they don't have the pain, that is *the* finding.
- ❌ Following up with a sales pitch within 48 hours. The
  follow-up is "here are my notes, did I get this right?"
- ❌ Counting the number of "would buy" responses. Count the
  number of *concrete past stories* and the number of
  *referrals*; those predict.

## E. Anti-patterns specific to this domain

- ❌ Anchoring on FDA / EMA / EU AI Act *vocabulary* before the
  interviewee has used those words. Let them lead with the
  framework they actually use.
- ❌ Demonstrating technical depth too early. The first 25
  minutes are listening; technical depth comes only if they
  ask.
- ❌ Promising clinical-grade certainty about anything. Phase 2
  is explicit that openMiura is not a clinical claim. Make
  sure that is also clear in the conversation.
