# Target personas

The six personas below are ranked by **information value per
30-minute conversation** for the discovery phase. The ranking
reflects "how much we learn about the pain we are trying to
address per hour of conversation", not "how likely they are to
sign a contract" — those are different questions answered later.

## P1 — QA Director, mid-size pharma

**Title:** Quality Assurance Director, or Head of Quality.
**Organisation:** mid-size pharmaceutical company (50–500 FTE in
QA across manufacturing + analytical + clinical).
**Jurisdiction:** EU GMP for manufacturing, often US 21 CFR
Part 11 alongside.

**Daily job:** sign-off on batch release decisions, custody of
the Quality Management System, supplier audits, response to
deviations and CAPAs. Owns the relationship with national
inspectorates and, indirectly, with EMA / FDA.

**Why valuable for discovery:** they make *the* decisions that
the Phase 2 mappings claim to support. If openMiura's
governance pattern is useful, the QA Director either
recognises the pattern as a clean version of what they
already build manually, or tells us we got the framing
wrong.

**Channels to find them:** ISPE, GAMP Forum, PDA (Parenteral
Drug Association) chapters, LinkedIn search. Often willing to
talk for 30 minutes if approached respectfully and not
pitched.

## P2 — CSV / CSA Validation Lead

**Title:** Validation Lead, Computerised System Validation
Manager, or (in companies that have moved on) Computer Software
Assurance Lead.
**Organisation:** any GxP-regulated company; often consultancies
that do CSV for several clients.

**Daily job:** writes URS / FS / DS / IQ / OQ / PQ; runs
risk-based scope decisions; defends the validation package
against inspectors.

**Why valuable for discovery:** Phase 2's V-model framing and
the migration path in §5.4 of the whitepaper land or fall in
this conversation. The CSV/CSA lead is the person who
converts "regulatory text" into "test cases and traceability
matrix".

**Channels:** ISPE chapters, validation conferences (ISPE
Annual Meeting, GAMP USA / Europe), LinkedIn.

## P3 — Head of QC Analytical (NMR / HPLC / GC-MS lab)

**Title:** Head of Analytical Quality Control, Lab Manager
analytical.
**Organisation:** pharmaceutical or contract research
organisation with an analytical services arm.

**Daily job:** runs the analytical bench; reviews
out-of-specification results, releases analytical reports for
batch release; owns instrument qualification and method
transfer.

**Why valuable:** the canonical use case in the whitepaper
(§6.1 analytical interpretation under QA approval) lands
here. They are the person who would put openMiura between
the analyst and the laboratory notebook.

**Channels:** ISPE QC group, ASMS for mass spec, EAS, and
any specific NMR / HPLC vendor user groups.

## P4 — Regulatory Affairs (RA) Officer

**Title:** Regulatory Affairs Manager / Officer, or
Compliance Officer.
**Organisation:** pharma, medical-device, or
combination-product manufacturer.

**Daily job:** prepares submissions to FDA / EMA / national
agencies; keeps the regulatory dossier coherent; handles
queries from the agencies.

**Why valuable:** they care about the *defensibility* of the
audit trail. Their answer to "would you defend this evidence
pack in front of an inspector" tells us whether the format and
content choices are right.

**Channels:** RAPS (Regulatory Affairs Professionals Society)
chapters, TOPRA in Europe, LinkedIn.

## P5 — IT-OT Industrial / Site IT Manager

**Title:** Site IT Manager, OT Engineering Lead, or
Manufacturing IT Manager.
**Organisation:** any GxP manufacturing site.

**Daily job:** runs the on-site computerised systems (LIMS,
MES, batch record systems, ELN); deals with vendor patches,
backups, disaster recovery; first-line owner of access
management.

**Why valuable:** they know whether openMiura would actually
*deploy* in their site. The honest answer to "what would it
take to put this behind your reverse proxy and into your
identity provider" is gold.

**Channels:** local user groups, LinkedIn search by site name
+ "IT", referrals from P1 (QA Director).

## P6 — Hospital service head with research line (UAL-style)

**Title:** Head of analytical / molecular biology / imaging
service inside a research hospital, who also leads a
clinical-research line.
**Organisation:** university hospital with active research
collaborations; in Curro's network: UAL collaborators in
chemistry / analytical service plus clinical-research
contacts.

**Daily job:** clinical service plus research projects; lives
the gap between hospital data systems and what a research
project actually needs to record.

**Why valuable:** Phase 2's three use cases (embryo,
colorectal, cardiovascular) live here. The conversation tells
us whether the governance pattern is welcome, frightening or
irrelevant.

**Channels:** Curro's existing UAL network, regional
biomedical research foundations (FIBHR, FIDIS, equivalents),
SEFH (Sociedad Española de Farmacia Hospitalaria) for the
hospital pharmacy angle.

---

## Ranking the conversations

For the *first ten* discovery conversations, we recommend two
each from P1, P2 and P3, plus one each from P4, P5, P6, and a
free slot for whoever offers their time first. Pattern
recognition starts at five conversations and is reasonably
robust at ten; aiming for ten in the first three weeks is
realistic.
